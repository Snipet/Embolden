## Title

Embolden – Reading Focus for ADHD

## Summary (132 chars max)

Bolds the start of every word to give your eyes fixation anchors. Read
faster and stay focused — designed with ADHD in mind.

## Description

**Reading online is hard when your attention keeps sliding off the page.**
Embolden bolds the first few letters of every word, giving your eyes a
steady series of anchors to land on. Your brain fills in the rest of each
word automatically — most people feel the difference within a paragraph.

**Why it helps**
Your eyes don't glide smoothly when you read; they hop from fixation point
to fixation point. By making the start of each word visually prominent,
Embolden gives every hop a clear landing spot. For many readers — especially
those with ADHD — that structure makes it far easier to keep momentum
through long articles, documentation, and news.

**Tuned to your eyes, not ours**
There's no single right amount of bold. Slide Anchor from a one-letter hint
to most of the word, set Ink anywhere from a light touch to full black, and
turn Variation up if a uniform prefix starts to feel like wallpaper — every
change previews live in the popup before it touches a page.

**Features**
• Works on the pages you already read — articles, docs, forums, web apps
• Three dials with a live preview: Anchor (how much of each word is bolded),
  Ink (how heavy the bold looks), and Variation (varies the anchor length
  word by word so the rhythm never flattens out)
• Per-site off switch (popup or Alt+B) that remembers your choice
• Settings sync across your Chrome profile
• Leaves code blocks, text fields, editors, and icons untouched
• Copy/paste, find-in-page, and screen readers keep working normally

**Private by design**
Embolden reads nothing and sends nothing. No analytics, no accounts, no
network requests — the only thing it stores is your own settings. The broad
site-access permission is simply what any extension that restyles pages
needs; page content never leaves your browser.

**Good to know**
Canvas-based apps (like Google Docs) and Chrome's built-in pages can't be
modified by extensions. The rare site that misbehaves is one Alt+B away
from being back to normal.
